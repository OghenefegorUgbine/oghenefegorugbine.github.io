01 / About

I'm a graduate mechanical engineer based in Saint-Étienne, France, with an Erasmus Mundus Joint MSc in Manufacturing 4.0 (META 4.0), completed across École Centrale de Lyon/ENISE, École des Mines de Saint-Étienne, and Politecnico di Torino, and a Bachelor of Engineering in Mechanical Engineering from Cross River University of Technology, Nigeria.

Over the past three years my work has moved between the mechanical design bench, the maintenance floor, and the simulation desk: designing and validating mechanical systems in SolidWorks, running static and dynamic FEA in Abaqus, and building a Python–Abaqus computational pipeline for machining simulation during my master's thesis at MISUTECH. Before that, I spent time keeping safety-critical mechanical systems running (LPG process lines, compressors, fire pumps, and water-treatment equipment) as a Maintenance Engineer at Techno Oil Limited, and diagnosing automotive mechanical and electrical faults at Edes Global Services.

My interest in sustainable industrial technology runs through most of what I do, from biodegradable-material selection in a 3D-printing design challenge to a personal research project applying a physics-informed neural network to accelerate structural simulation, and a fellowship building a solar decision-support tool for African contexts. I'm looking for graduate and research-track engineering roles where I can keep working across that same range (mechanical design, computational mechanics, manufacturing, and maintenance & reliability), ideally with a strong sustainability angle.

MISUTECH · Master's Thesis · Mar–Sept 2025

Inverse Identification of Mechanical Behaviour Laws for Machining Simulation

An inverse-identification method for calibrating material behaviour laws used in numerical machining simulation. Combines finite element modelling of orthogonal cutting in Abaqus with a Python-driven Levenberg–Marquardt optimisation loop that systematically adjusts Johnson–Cook constitutive parameters and the Taylor–Quinney coefficient against experimental chip-thickness, contact-length, cutting-force, and penetration-force data.

Average error across 4 validation metrics fell from 21–41% to 0.14–4.35%, typically within 5 iterations
PythonAbaqus/ExplicitFEMJohnson–CookLevenberg–Marquardt

Independent Research · March 2026

AIIM: FEM-PINN Surrogate for Nonlinear Solid Mechanics

A Python elastoplastic FEM solver modelling large-deformation structural behaviour, used to generate training data for a Physics-Informed Neural Network that predicts full-field stress, strain, and displacement directly.
